# Problem Statement

## Background

Cybersecurity teams continuously receive indicators of compromise (IOCs), including
IP addresses, domains, URLs, file hashes, and incident signals. These indicators may
come from multiple feeds, email reports, endpoint tools, and analyst investigations.
The volume and variety of this information make it difficult to review every signal
consistently and quickly.

## The Problem

Security teams need a centralized way to submit, classify, prioritize, and investigate
threat indicators. Without a common workflow, analysts must manually compare indicators,
estimate risk, identify related attack techniques, and decide what action to take. This
can cause important threats to be overlooked while low-priority alerts consume valuable
investigation time.

## Who is Affected

The primary users are security analysts, incident responders, and small security
operations teams that need a fast triage workflow but may not have a large collection
of specialized threat intelligence tools. The platform is also useful for students,
demonstrators, and security engineers who need to explain why an indicator is considered
risky.

## Why It Matters

Inconsistent triage increases the time required to understand an alert and can delay
containment actions. Analysts may also lose context when indicator findings are spread
across separate tools or notes. A clear risk score, explanation, and recommended action
help teams focus attention on the most urgent indicators first and make their decisions
more consistent.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Existing workflows often combine separate threat feeds, spreadsheets, browser searches,
ticketing systems, and incident response tools. These approaches can provide useful
information, but they do not always offer one place to analyze an IOC, compare severity,
view related incidents, and track response status. The result is a fragmented workflow
with limited explainability and repeated manual effort.
